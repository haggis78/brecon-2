Letters Patent of Henry VIII

Background

Letters Patent are a type of document issued by an authority, particularly a monarch. "Patent", meaning "open or plain", distinguishes them from Letters Close, which were issued rolled up, with the wax seal serving as a guarantee to the recipient that no-one had opened them to read their contents.

English (later British) monarchs issued Letters Patent in large numbers, particularly to grant rights, privileges, or property. To authenticate the document, the Great Seal, the monarch's largest and most official seal, was set into a wax disc, through which passed a cord or strip of parchment that in turn passed through slits or holes at the bottom of the parchment on which the document was written. Other examples of Letters Patent, including one from 1546, can be seen here.

Because of the number of Letters Patent, and their importance as a source to historians, they are calendared (listed with summaries, but not with full edited texts) in the series Calendar of Patent Rolls.
