Reading views

Among the advantages of a digital edition is the ability to take the same base XML file and write as many XSLT stylesheets as one might wish to create a multitude of reading views. One of the purposes of this particular edition is to do so in an experimental manner, trying out different formats. These are listed here.

Manuscript images with transcription

We did not secure permission to include the images of all manuscripts on this site, but The National Archives of the United Kingdom kindly gave permission to include images of the Patent Roll (C 66). These are provided here, broken into sections, with line-by-line transcriptions. The "show/hide" button under each image gives users the opportunity to use this page as a palaeography exercise: our transcription can be hidden while you make your own, and then revealed so you may compare your readings with those given on the site.

Comparison views

This page allows users to select any three versions of the text, using the checkboxes at the top. Sections where there are differences among texts--not necessarily among the three versions you selected, but among any versions--are given with red text; black text indicates where all copies agree.

The text was also published in translation by Theophilus Jones. In due course there will be a reading version with Jones's edition of the Latin text (our Witness J) in parallel columns with his translation. Jones published these in different works, and they have never been brought together to allow readers to compare the Latin and English versions.

Reading views of individual copies

Eleven copies of the text were collated for this project: three sixteenth-century copies created during the process of issuing the Letters Patent; five seventeenth- and eighteenth-century transcriptions, four of them being manuscripts of the Statutes of St Davids Cathedral in which the Brecon Letters Patent appear as an appendix; and three antiquarian printings in the eighteenth to early nineteenth centuries. Users can read any one copy by itself by following the links below.

While these pages preserve many of the unique readings of each individual copy, they are not quite diplomatic editions. The sixteenth-century copies, from with all the others are derived, were heavily abbreviated; and unlike most medieval Latin manuscripts, the scribes who prepared these did not use specific standardized abbreviations that communicated to the reader the exact letters being omitted. Often a word is cut short with a tic resembling an apostrophe; many of the differences among the later copies result from different readers using their knowledge of Latin grammar to fill in plausible endings and coming to different conclusions. In our XML, and therefore in these pages, if the early copies abbreviate but all later copies agree on the expansion, the expanded version is given.

Sixteenth-century manuscript copies

  • Witness Y: Privy Seal Warrant: Constituted both the first draft and the king's authorization for Chancery to prepare its own warrant
  • Witness Z: Warrant in Chancery: Prepared from Y; constituted both the draft and authorization for Chancery to issue the Letters Patent under the Great Seal of Henry VIII
  • Witness R: Chancery Patent Roll: The official file copy of the Letters Patent, but clearly created by copying from Z, not from the Letters Patent document issued with the Great Seal

Seventeenth- and eighteenth-century manuscript copies

  • Witness B: Copy made and kept at Christ College Brecon
  • Witness C: Appendix to Statutes of St Davids; copy at St John's College, Cambridge
  • Witness D: Appendix to Statutes of St Davids; among St Davids Cathedral manuscripts now at National Library of Wales
  • Witness I: Appendix to Statutes of St Davids; among St Davids Cathedral manuscripts now at National Library of Wales
  • Witness O: Appendix to Statutes of St Davids; in Harley Manuscripts at the British Library

Antiquarian printings

  • Witness W: First printing; from Browne Willis's An History of the Mitred Parliamentary Abbies II (1719)
  • Witness S: Cites W as source; from John Stevens's History of the antient abbeys II (1723)
  • Witness J: Cites S as source; from Theophilus Jones's A History of the County of Brecknock II (1809/1898)
