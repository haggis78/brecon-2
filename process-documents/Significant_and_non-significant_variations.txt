Significant and non-significant reading variations in CharterCollation.xml

Certain variations have been judged not to be significant (in the proper sense of the term: meaning-making). They say more about the preferences of an individual scribe than they do about accuracy or copying. The following are generally insignificant with exceptions as noted:

Punctuation: Since we began with a typescript of W, we left that normalized punctuation intact. Some printings and manuscripts include accent marks (grave, acute, and circumflex), which have all been silently omitted.

Ampersands have all been silently expanded to 'et'.

Capitalization

i/j: Variations between these have not even been marked; we began with a typescript of witness W, using a normalized i/j distinction (j when used as a consonant, i as a vowel) as was already common in the early 18th century, and MS witnesses BCDIO and printings WSJ all follow this consistently. The 16th-century MSS, witnesses RYZ, do not follow this consonant/vowel distinction, instead using j (if at all) when it is the second of a double, as in Collegij.

æ/e and œ/e distinction (the c16th MSS all use e, in medieval form; that later MSS and printings all use ae/oe, in classical/Renaissance form); EXCEPT in a non-Latin word (ymaendny, Escaetorum) in which ae is used in the c16th MSS. In those latter cases, the XML uses -ae- rather than -æ- to signify that difference in usage. The æ and œ digraphs are transformed into e only for the 16th-century MSS in the XSLT using a string function, so they appear in the TEI XML but are changed to e in the HTML output for those MSS only.

quecu'que/quecumque/quecunque (and their grammatical parallels)

ac eciam/aceciam/ac etiam: these have been tagged as apps, but mark them as insignificant

ante hac/antehac: these have been tagged as apps, but mark them as insignificant

quoquo modo/quoquomodo: these have been tagged as apps, but mark them as insignificant

most c/t/s distinctions in -cio/-tio/-sio formations, e.g.:
	servicia
	servitia
or:
	reversiones
	revertiones
	reverciones
EXCEPT when some witness/es include a different word altogether at that same place (e.g. reventiones) which needs to be marked as a point of divergence.

messuagia/mesuagia: these have been tagged as apps, but mark them as insignificant

Types of changes that ARE treated as significant:
-Missing, added, or changed words
-use of synonyms: ac/et; sanctum/sacrum
-transpositions of words
-different spellings of personal names: Ricardus/Richardus, Willelmo/Gulielmo
-different spellings of place names: various forms of Brecknok; Abergwilly
-different grammatical forms: mood (possint/possunt); singular/plural; case
